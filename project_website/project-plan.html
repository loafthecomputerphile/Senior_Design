COVID-19 Acoustic Detector

A cough classifier that screens a recording and returns a COVID / healthy probability — trained offline, run on-device.

Team

Jane Doe · Sam Rivera · Alex Chen

Advisor

Philip Chan — cs.fit.edu/~pkc

Domain

Signal Processing · ML · Screening

Objective

Build an affordable, minimally invasive screening layer for respiratory illness.

PCR testing is accurate but centralized, slow, and expensive per capita. We want something that reaches farther: a cough you already produce, recorded on a phone, and read by a small neural network trained to recognize a COVID-linked spectral signature. A flagged result routes to a clinical test; a clean one saves a swab. The system is explicitly a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

[01] Problem

Screening is gated behind hardware and clinics. A mic is universal. Translating cough acoustics into a defensible probability is the open problem; the data is noisy, class-balance is skewed, and audio is untrusted at the edge.

[02] Related Work

Public cough corpora (COVID-19 Sounds, Coswara) and prior cough-classification studies. We reuse the spectrogram + CNN convention, but stress VAD robustness and an honest AUC rather than a headline number.

[03] Plan

Semester A

  • · Collect & label cough corpora
  • · Preprocess: VAD, trim, resample
  • · Feature extract: mel-spectrogram / MFCC
  • · Baseline CNN + evaluation harness
  • · Midpoint review with advisor

Semester B

  • · Tune CNN / LSTM, dropout regularize
  • · Robustness: augments, mic variance
  • · Ship on-device inference (ONNX)
  • · Evidence: poster, manuals, video
  • · Final review + public demo

[05] Risk & Mitigation

Noisy labels

Clean + rebalance corpora; report AUC not raw acc.

Overhyped result

Held-out split, no leakage; publish honest thresholds.

Edge-case audio

Test on unknown mics/rooms; the demo labels itself a mock.

Privacy

On-device inference only. Audio is not retained.
